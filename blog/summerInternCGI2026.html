My Experience as a Summer Intern at CGI

Posted 17 July 2026. Last updated 17 July 2026

A reflection on my summer internship at CGI UK during June-July 2026.

This internship journey began with my friend telling me about the 10,000 Black Interns Programme a year ago. The 10,000 Interns Foundation, which runs the programme, is a charity which aims to tackle underrepresentation by matching young black people with paid internship opportunities. My friend had done a summer internship through the programme and recommended that I apply when they next opened applications. So, in October 2025, I applied for an internship in both technical and non-technical technology fields. I was matched with CGI, had an online interview and got the role!

What I Did

I began my internship in early June, which lasted for seven weeks until mid-July. I was assigned to a pro-bono project with another intern, Tio, to design and develop an IT solution for the Salford Foundation, a charity based in Manchester. They needed a system to manage their files to support compliance with the Data Protection Act and GDPR, as their existing systems were scattered across various platforms and data was at risk of being stored for too long or left untracked.

During the first two weeks, we did our research on both the Salford Foundation and the work they do, as well as getting insights from the staff. We had several interviews with different members of staff across the charity, asking about their current workflows to handle consent gathering, file storage and organisation, as well as their opinions on the current systems. This gave us a deep look into what was currently working well and what needed to be improved, which made it easier to cement our requirements. To visualise a possible solution for the Salford Foundation, I created mock-up designs in Figma to map out important pages that would be needed in the app.

After collecting all of our findings, we presented it to the charity and proposed our solution: a custom web app that utilised Microsoft SharePoint APIs to host files separately from the app itself, to save costs on dedicated servers for file storage by utilising their existing Microsoft 365 subscription.

We went forward with this design for the beginning of our development for the next two weeks. Throughout development we were offered lots of support from others at CGI — engaging in great conversations with seasoned professionals who could offer their experience to us. We also had daily stand up meetings with CGI's Justice AI Squad during this development period, giving them updates and getting advice on how to use AI for software development and testing.

As development wound down, we moved onto testing the application and getting direct feedback from the charity. This is an essential phase as we could present them with a version of the app, and they can provide suggestions or modifications. From that feedback, we could go back and improve the app ready for the next demo. Agile development is all about iterative processes, a constant loop of improving upon the previous version to create a product that the client wants and most importantly: actually uses.

In the final week, we handed over the finished application to the Salford Foundation and gave a presentation to their senior management team about our work and our experience as interns. I hope that the application becomes a useful tool for many of the staff there. I would like to thank everyone at the Salford Foundation for their enthusiasm and constant engagement throughout — they made working on this project a delight!

What I Learnt

One thing I learnt during development is that there is a big difference between projects at university versus this project at CGI. You can read more about my university projects here, but all of them have one key trait: once I've finished with the assignment, the project is done and I get a grade a few weeks or months later. This is not true for software developed for a client, where the completion of development is only the beginning of the software's life: it must usually be maintained and updated for several years (maybe even decades!) afterwards.

Thus, throughout both the design and development stages I had to consider not only if the app would work as-is, but also if it would continue to work in the future. What happens when lots of files are on the system? I had to also know what questions to ask: will there be hundreds of files? Thousands? Millions? Knowing the requirements of your client becomes essential here. There is no point in creating an elaborate, expensive system that can handle millions or billions of data records if only a few hundred is needed at most. I had to balance these requirements with cost and time, making sure I gave the client what they wanted, within the price range, and create it within a suitable time frame. While crunching for deadlines can be tough during university, there is a lot more guidance on what needs to be done, or even individual deadlines for each part of an assignment to make it easier to break down the required tasks.

There is also a difference between presenting to a client versus academic staff. At university, there is often a focus on what you know and proving that you know it, often through technical language and detailed explanations. My client, however, was not technical at all. Concepts such as APIs, web hosting and databases needed to be simplified and abstracted from technical jargon, to be understandable without losing important information. I gained a lot of practice in this through the several meetings I had over the course of my internship, and that effort we took in communicating clearly and effectively meant that staff would always be on the same page as us interns.

Working at CGI

The environment at CGI was always welcoming and I settled in quickly at their London office. I found support from staff in person and online through various Teams channels, participated in events, and networked with others in the office. Many of the people I spoke to had been at the company for several years — five, ten, fifteen years and beyond — and one of the most common reasons I heard for why they had stayed for so long was the community. From my experience at CGI, I can clearly see why!

CGI offers many networks that supports the diverse range of people, for young professionals, neurodiversity, and cultural networks just to name a few. I attended panels, online chats and in person meetups, getting the opportunity to meet with people from all over the UK that I may not have been able to otherwise.

My cohort was the first group of summer interns at CGI UK, which meant it was a learning experience on both sides. Despite that, everything was well organised and coordinated, so I was never left idle! Each intern was paired up and assigned different projects, so everyone was doing something unique while not working alone. My feedback was sought out at regular opportunities through a call at the start of the week and a report to write at the end, which helped me to keep track of everything I had done during my time there.

If you are considering an early careers position at CGI, be it an internship, placement or graduate role — I certainly recommend it.

What Next?

I still have two more years of university to go. I'll carry forward the experience and knowledge that I've gained in these last seven weeks into the rest of my degree, whether it be my third year project or any group assignments. I've learnt so much in such a short amount of time, and I must thank CGI, the Salford Foundation and the 10000 Interns Foundation for such an incredible opportunity!
